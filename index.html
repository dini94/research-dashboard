1 Research Background & Introduction

Global energy consumption continues to escalate, driving the urgency for sustainable and clean photovoltaic technologies. Dye-Sensitized Solar Cells (DSSCs) have received widespread attention due to their eco-friendliness, ease of fabrication, transparency flexibility, and superior indoor light-harvesting potential.

The molecular sensitizer is the heart of a DSSC, governing photon absorption and interfacial electron injection into the semiconductor (typically $\text{TiO}_2$). Traditional ruthenium(II)-based complexes suffer from limited availability, environmental toxicity, and high processing costs. Consequently, metal-free organic dyes adopting the donor-$\pi$-acceptor ($D-\pi-A$) or donor-auxiliary acceptor-$\pi$-acceptor ($D-A_2-\pi-A_1$) frameworks have emerged as compelling alternatives.

Designing novel sensitizers experimentally through trial-and-error chemical synthesis is time-consuming and resource-intensive. Computational quantum chemistry—specifically Density Functional Theory (DFT) and Time-Dependent Density Functional Theory (TD-DFT)—provides an invaluable *in-silico* platform to predict frontier molecular orbital (FMO) distributions, optical absorption band structures, charge-transfer dynamics, and theoretical photovoltaic metrics prior to wet-lab synthesis.

4 Theoretical & Photovoltaic Governing Equations

Quantum Chemical Descriptors (Koopmans' Theorem)

$$\text{Energy Gap: } E_{gap} = E_{LUMO} - E_{HOMO}$$

$$\text{Ionization Potential: } IP \approx -E_{HOMO}$$

$$\text{Electron Affinity: } EA \approx -E_{LUMO}$$

$$\text{Electronegativity: } \chi = \frac{IP + EA}{2}$$

$$\text{Chemical Hardness: } \eta = \frac{IP - EA}{2}$$

$$\text{Electrophilicity Index: } \omega = \frac{\chi^2}{2\eta}$$

Optical & Photovoltaic Parameters

$$\text{Light Harvesting Efficiency: } LHE = 1 - 10^{-f}$$

where $f$ is the oscillator strength from TD-DFT calculation.

$$\text{Open-Circuit Voltage: } V_{oc} = \frac{E_{LUMO} - E_{CB}(\text{TiO}_2)}{e} - \Delta V_{shift}$$

$$\text{Power Conversion Efficiency: } PCE = \frac{J_{sc} \times V_{oc} \times FF}{P_{in}} \times 100\%$$
